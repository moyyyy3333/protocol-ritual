Peptide Dosing Made Simple

Understanding your dose doesn't require a medical degree. Here's everything you need to know to dose accurately and safely.

1

Know Your Vial Strength

Every peptide vial has a labeled strength (e.g., 10 mg). This is the total amount of peptide powder in the vial before reconstitution. Always confirm this before adding BAC water.

2

Add BAC Water Correctly

Use bacteriostatic water (0.9% benzyl alcohol). The amount you add determines the concentration. Common volumes: 1 ml, 2 ml, or 3 ml depending on vial size. Less water = stronger concentration per unit.

3

Calculate Concentration

Divide vial strength by BAC volume: 10 mg ÷ 2 ml = 5 mg/ml. This is your base concentration. Every 0.1 ml (10 units on a 1 ml insulin syringe) draws 0.5 mg at this concentration.

4

Standard GLP-1 Starting Doses

Tirzepatide: 2.5 mg/week (4 weeks), then 5 mg. Semaglutide: 0.25 mg/week (4 weeks), then 0.5 mg. Retatrutide: 2 mg/week (4 weeks), then 4 mg. Always follow your provider's protocol.

5

Insulin Syringe Units

A standard 1 ml insulin syringe has 100 units. 1 unit = 0.01 ml. Example: to draw 0.5 mg from a 5 mg/ml concentration, you need 10 units (0.1 ml).

Peptide Storage & Protocol Guides

Storage

How to Store BPC-157 for Maximum Potency

BPC-157 degrades in light within hours. Reconstituted vials last 14-21 days refrigerated. Freeze lyophilized powder at -20°C for 12+ months.

Travel

Flying with Peptides: TSA Guide 2026

TSA allows peptides in carry-on. Keep vials in original packaging, use a hard case, and carry your prescription or research documentation.
